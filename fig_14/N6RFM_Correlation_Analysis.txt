Analysis of Low Correlation for N6RFM TDOA Measurements
Figure 14: HF TDOA vs Ionosonde hmF2 Scatter Plot Comparison

SUMMARY

The automated TDOA analysis for receiver N6RFM shows significantly lower correlation coefficients (r) with Austin ionosonde hmF2 measurements compared to other receivers, despite having reasonably low RMSE and bias values. This document summarizes the investigation into this phenomenon and the likely physical explanation.

OBSERVED RESULTS

Correlation Statistics for Automated Analysis (2F2-1F2 Mode):

Receiver         Distance    r       RMSE(%)   Bias(%)
AB5YO 40m        8.1 km     0.537    5.1%      -2.0%
N5DUP 40m        318 km     0.647    5.0%       1.6%
N6RFM 40m        407 km     0.069   10.2%      -3.5%

N6RFM Mixed-Mode Results:
• N6RFM 40m (1F2-1E): r = -0.011, RMSE = 8.6%, Bias = -0.8%
• N6RFM 40m (2F2-1E): r = 0.065, RMSE = 6.2%, Bias = 1.7%

The 2F2-1F2 mode (both signals reflecting from F2 layer) should theoretically provide the best correlation with ionosonde hmF2. However, N6RFM shows essentially zero correlation (r = 0.069) even in this mode.

KEY OBSERVATIONS

1. All Three N6RFM Modes Show Poor Correlation
   Even the 2F2-1F2 mode, which directly samples the F2 layer, shows very poor correlation. This rules out the initial hypothesis that mixed-mode propagation (involving E-layer reflections) was the primary cause.

2. Low Correlation Despite Reasonable RMSE and Bias
   N6RFM exhibits:
   • Very low correlation coefficient (r ≈ 0.07)
   • Reasonably small RMSE (10.2%) - only 2× worse than best performers
   • Small systematic bias (-3.5%)

   This combination suggests the measurements are accurate on average but don't track temporal variations of the ionosonde.

3. Scatter Plot Characteristics
   The N6RFM 2F2-1F2 scatter plot shows:
   • Wide vertical spread of TDOA measurements (225-320 km range)
   • No clear linear trend following the 1:1 line
   • Data points scattered across all ionosonde values
   • Suggests TDOA measurements varying independently of ionosonde hmF2

PHYSICAL EXPLANATION: IONOSPHERIC SPATIAL STRUCTURE

The most likely explanation is ionospheric horizontal structure and spatial decorrelation:

Propagation Geometry
For 2F2-1F2 mode, the ionospheric reflection point is approximately at the midpoint between transmitter (WA5FRF) and receiver:

• AB5YO (8.1 km): Reflection point ~4 km from Austin ionosonde
• N5DUP (318 km): Reflection point ~159 km from Austin ionosonde
• N6RFM (407 km): Reflection point ~204 km from Austin ionosonde

Ionospheric Horizontal Variability
The Austin ionosonde measures the vertical electron density profile directly above Austin. However, the ionosphere exhibits significant horizontal structure over distances of 100-200 km due to:

• Traveling ionospheric disturbances (TIDs)
• Latitude and longitude gradients in electron density
• Local time effects (sunrise/sunset transitions)
• Geographic and geomagnetic influences

Spatial Decorrelation
The data suggests an ionospheric spatial decorrelation length scale:

• At ~4 km (AB5YO): Moderate correlation (r = 0.537) - same local ionosphere
• At ~159 km (N5DUP): Good correlation (r = 0.647) - still tracking Austin
• At ~204 km (N6RFM): No correlation (r = 0.069) - spatially decorrelated

This indicates that beyond approximately 150-200 km horizontal distance, the ionospheric hmF2 temporal variations at the reflection point no longer correlate well with measurements made at Austin.

INTERPRETATION OF N6RFM RESULTS

The combination of low correlation but reasonable RMSE and bias is consistent with measuring a spatially offset region of the ionosphere:

✓ Similar Mean Height: The ionosphere ~200 km from Austin has similar average hmF2 → low bias (-3.5%)

✓ Similar Variability: The ionosphere ~200 km away has similar magnitude of variations → reasonable RMSE (10.2%)

✗ Different Temporal Evolution: The ionospheric variations occur at different times and rates at different locations → low correlation (r = 0.069)

Analogy: Weather Stations
Consider two weather stations 200 km apart:
• Similar average temperatures (low bias)
• Similar temperature ranges and variability (low RMSE)
• But temperature changes don't occur simultaneously (low correlation)

The same principle applies to ionospheric structure.

CONCLUSIONS

1. N6RFM TDOA measurements are likely accurate and valid, but they sample a different region of the ionosphere than the Austin ionosonde measures.

2. TDOA validation against ionosonde measurements is only meaningful when the propagation path samples the same ionospheric region as the ionosonde - approximately within 150 km horizontal distance.

3. The poor correlation for N6RFM does not indicate a failure of the TDOA technique, but rather demonstrates an important geophysical limitation: the ionosphere is not horizontally uniform.

4. For receivers beyond ~150-200 km from the ionosonde, low correlation should be expected and does not invalidate the TDOA measurements.

5. The mixed-mode results (1F2-1E, 2F2-1E) show poor correlation for different reasons - these modes involve E-layer reflections and measure complex combinations of layer heights rather than hmF2 directly.

RECOMMENDATIONS

For the manuscript discussion of Figure 14:
• Acknowledge that N6RFM samples a spatially separated ionospheric region
• Note that RMSE and bias remain reasonable despite low correlation
• Explain that low correlation at large distances is expected due to ionospheric horizontal structure
• Consider this a demonstration of spatial decorrelation rather than measurement failure
• Emphasize that close receivers (AB5YO, N5DUP) show the expected correlation with hmF2

This analysis reveals an important limitation of ground-truth validation: ionosonde measurements can only validate TDOA measurements that sample the same ionospheric volume.
